What I focus on

Most of my work sits before the modelling step. In computational biology the quality of a result depends on the quality of the data behind it — where it came from, what each column means, what units it uses, and what was deliberately left out. So my focus is on:

  • Protein sequence and structure analysis using predicted models
  • Residue-level feature extraction with fully documented definitions and units
  • Building validated, reproducible datasets from public structure databases
  • Understanding the confidence measures that come with predicted structures

How I approach the work

My public repositories document a consistent set of practices, and this is the approach I try to hold to:

  • Recompute rather than trust. Integrity tests recalculate critical values from the raw prediction files instead of accepting derived outputs.
  • Document definitions and units. Every feature column is defined in a machine-readable schema, so a number means the same thing to everyone reading it.
  • Record exclusions explicitly. When data is unavailable, the column is excluded and the reason is written down — not imputed or silently redefined.
  • Inspect before changing. Read-only review comes first; findings get reported rather than fixed automatically.
  • Report the limits alongside the result. Every project states what it does not do, and null or negative outcomes stay on the record.

Where I am right now

I am early in this work, and I would rather say so plainly. My projects are at the pipeline and documentation stage — not at the stage of finished results. What exists today is validated data infrastructure, test suites, and technical writing. What does not exist yet is a trained model, a biological finding, or a publication.

If you are working in computational biology or machine learning and see something worth correcting or building on, I would genuinely like to hear it.
